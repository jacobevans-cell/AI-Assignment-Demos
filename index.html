GAMIFIED MATH

Decimal Deception

ACTIVITY DESCRIPTION

Students enter a playable space-station adventure built around decimal operations. They choose a character, navigate rooms, avoid enemies, restore six Power Crystals through math missions, earn points, preserve lives, unlock portals, and progress through themed levels.

ARIZONA STANDARD

6.NS.B.3
Fluently add, subtract, multiply, and divide multi-digit decimals using the standard algorithm.

WHAT WAS BUILT
  • Five selectable student characters
  • Six Power Crystal math missions
  • Lives, enemies, scoring, portals, and themed level progression
  • Problem-specific Math Scanner hints and permanent Decimal Guide
INTENTIONAL DESIGN

The goal was not to remove the decimal practice. The goal was to redesign the experience surrounding it. Students still have to solve the mathematics correctly, but those problems now control their progress through a larger interactive mission.

The worksheet did not disappear. It became the engine of the experience.

INTERACTIVE MULTIMEDIA LESSON

Volleyball Academy

ACTIVITY DESCRIPTION

Students complete eight role-based learning missions covering positions, responsibilities, strategy, fundamentals, and film study. Missions combine deeper reading, real game situations, multimedia, comprehension checks, and application.

ARIZONA ELA ALIGNMENT

Grades 4–5 RI.1 & RI.7
Students use information from informational text, visuals, and multimedia sources to demonstrate understanding.

WHAT WAS BUILT
  • Eight instructional missions
  • 5–7 understanding checks per section
  • Embedded videos, readings, multiple choice, and drag-and-drop
  • Saved progress, scoring, feedback, mastery, and high scores
INTENTIONAL DESIGN

This began as separate web articles, instructional readings, and YouTube videos. Instead of sending students between resources, the content was transformed into one sequenced assignment requiring students to read, watch, respond, sort, decide, and apply what they learned.

Volleyball is simply the example. The same structure could be built around almost any content.

RESPONSIVE MATH SCAFFOLD

Long Division Quest

ACTIVITY DESCRIPTION

Students solve long division directly inside an aligned division structure, one digit and one decision at a time. The program guides students through Divide → Multiply → Subtract → Bring Down while preserving the actual algorithm.

ARIZONA STANDARD

5.NBT.B.6
Find whole-number quotients using place value, properties of operations, and multiplication/division relationships.

WHAT WAS BUILT
  • Aligned digit-by-digit input
  • Divide → Multiply → Subtract → Bring Down coaching
  • Visual grouping hints explaining quotient decisions
  • XP, accuracy, streaks, ratings, missions, and hint tracking
INTENTIONAL DESIGN

A traditional worksheet often reveals only that the final quotient is incorrect. This activity watches the student's process and responds to the specific digit and step where the thinking breaks down.

The scaffold responds to the process, not simply the final answer.

MULTIMODAL WORD STUDY

Spelling Practice Lab

ACTIVITY DESCRIPTION

One spelling list becomes a complete study-and-practice system. Students study instructional flash cards, hear words and sentences, practice through volleyball or soccer gameplay, and finish with an independent spelling test.

ARIZONA ELA ALIGNMENT

Grades 4–5 RF.3 & L.2
Apply grade-level phonics, word-analysis skills, language conventions, and accurate spelling.

WHAT WAS BUILT
  • Syllable breakdowns and whole-word pronunciation
  • Clickable Sound It Out chunks with individual audio
  • Automatically highlighted multi-letter phonograms
  • Watch For hints, visuals, examples, games, and testing
INTENTIONAL DESIGN

This was built beyond simple memorization. Students can see the structure of a word, hear its pronunciation, isolate sound chunks, identify phonograms, study spelling patterns, practice in context, and then demonstrate independence.

See it → hear it → break it apart → study it → practice it → spell it independently.

WORLD LANGUAGE • DIFFERENTIATED PRACTICE

Spanish Conversation Quest

ACTIVITY DESCRIPTION

Students build practical Spanish through short real-world conversation missions. They study vocabulary, hear pronunciation, complete listening tasks, construct sentences, interpret meaning, choose conversational responses, and apply language in realistic contexts.

WORLD LANGUAGE ALIGNMENT

Students practice the core communication modes of listening, interpretation, interpersonal communication, vocabulary development, and sentence construction.

WHAT WAS BUILT
  • Beginner, Standard, and Challenge support levels
  • Four real-world conversation missions
  • Vocabulary study, pronunciation, listening, recall, and sentence building
  • XP, streaks, mastery, immediate feedback, and reflection
INTENTIONAL DESIGN

The same conversational objective can be experienced at different levels of support. Beginner learners receive more English and pronunciation assistance, while Challenge mode reduces support and increases independence.

Same communication goal. Different levels of scaffolding.

PHYSICAL EDUCATION • ADAPTIVE CIRCUITS

Movement Mission

ACTIVITY DESCRIPTION

Students build a personalized K–8 movement circuit and move through timed physical-education stations. The activity provides coach cues, technique tips, rest periods, movement knowledge checks, reflection, progress tracking, XP, and achievements.

PHYSICAL EDUCATION ALIGNMENT

Supports movement competency, physical fitness, body control, movement knowledge, goal setting, reflection, and responsible participation.

WHAT WAS BUILT
  • K–2, 3–5, and 6–8 grade-band options
  • Explorer, Athlete, and Elite challenge levels
  • Equipment-aware and space-aware circuits
  • Adapted movement, timers, coach cues, reflections, XP, records, and teacher quick-launch
INTENTIONAL DESIGN

This activity demonstrates customization that goes beyond changing colors or themes. The actual student experience can change based on grade band, challenge level, available equipment, available space, and movement needs.

Same PE goals. Different training flavors.

THE EXPLORE CONNECTION

Build the Flavor

Explore's model recognizes that students do not all need the exact same experience to reach the same academic destination. AI gives teachers another way to extend that idea. Preserve the standard and rigor, while customizing the context, scaffolding, feedback, presentation, challenge, or type of interaction.

1. Keep the Target

Start with the standard, skill, text, vocabulary, assessment target, movement objective, or learning goal.

→
2. Design the Flavor

Change the theme, context, interaction, level of scaffolding, modality, difficulty, or student pathway.

→
3. Reach the Same Mastery

The student experience may be different, but the underlying learning expectation remains intentional and measurable.

📖 🎥 🎮

Same target. Different path.

A student working on decimals, vocabulary, reading comprehension, Spanish conversation, or a PE movement objective does not necessarily need the exact same experience as every other student. AI can make those different pathways far more practical for teachers to design, revise, reuse, and personalize.

How were these built?

The process begins with instructional design, not programming.

STEP 1
Start with the learning

Identify the standard, student need, existing resource, practice problem, lesson, assessment, or instructional challenge.

STEP 2
Design the experience

Decide what students should do, where support is needed, how feedback should work, and which flavor could make the learning more meaningful.

STEP 3
Build, test & revise

AI helps build and revise the technology. The teacher continues testing, adjusting rigor, refining directions, improving scaffolds, and deciding whether the experience actually supports learning.
