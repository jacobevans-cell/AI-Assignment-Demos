What happens when we take the curriculum we already teach and rethink how students experience it?

Four working examples of standards-based lessons, practice, scaffolding, and instructional content redesigned with AI.

Explore already asks us to think beyond a single one-size-fits-all classroom experience. These projects explore how AI can help us take that philosophy even further. GAMIFIED MATH

Decimal Deception

ACTIVITY DESCRIPTION

Students enter a playable space-station adventure built around decimal operations. They choose a character, navigate rooms, avoid moving enemies, restore six Power Crystals by completing math missions, earn points, preserve lives, unlock the portal, and progress into new themed levels.

ARIZONA STANDARD

6.NS.B.3
Fluently add, subtract, multiply, and divide multi-digit decimals using the standard algorithm.

WHAT WAS BUILT
  • Five selectable student characters
  • Six math missions tied to Power Crystals
  • Lives, enemies, scoring, portal progression, and themed levels
  • Math Scanner support and immediate answer feedback
INTENTIONAL DESIGN

The goal was not to make decimal practice easier. It was to make students willing to do more of it. Traditional practice problems became the mechanism for progressing through the game, so the mathematics still controls success.

The worksheet did not disappear. It became the engine of the experience.

INTERACTIVE MULTIMEDIA LESSON

Volleyball Academy

ACTIVITY DESCRIPTION

Students complete eight role-based learning missions covering libero, setter, outside hitter, middle blocker, defensive specialist, bench responsibilities, volleyball basics, and film study. Each mission combines deeper reading, real game situations, multimedia, comprehension checks, and application.

ARIZONA ELA ALIGNMENT

Grades 4–5 RI.1 & RI.7
Use evidence from informational text and integrate information from text, visual, and multimedia sources.

WHAT WAS BUILT
  • Eight complete instructional missions
  • 5–7 understanding checks per mission
  • Reading, embedded video, multiple choice, and drag-and-drop
  • Saved progress, scoring, feedback, high scores, and reset tools
INTENTIONAL DESIGN

Separate articles, instructional information, and videos were transformed into one structured experience requiring students to read, watch, analyze, respond, sort, and apply information.

Volleyball is the content. The design model can be used with almost any subject.

GUIDED MATH PRACTICE

Long Division Quest

ACTIVITY DESCRIPTION

Students complete long division directly inside an aligned division structure one digit and one decision at a time. The program guides Divide → Multiply → Subtract → Bring Down while preserving the actual algorithm.

ARIZONA STANDARD

5.NBT.B.6
Find whole-number quotients using place value, properties of operations, and multiplication/division relationships.

WHAT WAS BUILT
  • Aligned digit-by-digit long-division input
  • D → M → S → B coaching sequence
  • Visual grouping hints
  • XP, accuracy, streaks, ratings, hints, and mission tracking
INTENTIONAL DESIGN

Instead of waiting until the final quotient is wrong, the activity responds to the exact place where student thinking breaks down. Support appears during the process rather than after it.

The scaffold responds to the student's process, not just the final answer.

MULTIMODAL WORD STUDY

Spelling Practice Lab

ACTIVITY DESCRIPTION

One spelling list becomes a complete study system. Students study detailed flash cards, practice through volleyball and soccer gameplay, hear words and sentences, and complete an independent spelling test.

ARIZONA ELA STANDARDS

4–5.RF.3 & 4–5.L.2
Apply phonics and word-analysis skills and demonstrate accurate grade-appropriate spelling.

WHAT WAS BUILT
  • Syllables and pronunciation support
  • Clickable Sound It Out chunks
  • Automatically highlighted multi-letter phonograms
  • Watch For hints, visuals, examples, games, and testing
INTENTIONAL DESIGN

The flash cards expose the structure of each word through syllables, sounds, phonograms, spelling patterns, pronunciation, definitions, examples, and targeted reminders before independent practice.

See it → hear it → break it apart → study it → practice it → spell it independently.

THE EXPLORE CONNECTION

Build the Flavor

Explore's model recognizes that students do not all need the exact same experience to reach the same academic destination. AI creates another way to extend that idea: preserve the standard and rigor while changing the context, scaffolding, feedback, presentation, or student experience.

1. Keep the Target

Start with the same standard, skill, text, assessment target, or learning objective.

→
2. Change the Flavor

Change the theme, context, interaction, support, presentation, or mode of practice.

→
3. Reach the Same Mastery

Different experiences still lead students toward the same academic understanding.

🚀 🐉 🏀
Interest-Based Flavors

Space, fantasy, sports, animals, cooking, racing, or another context built around student interest.

📖 🎥 🎮
Learning-Mode Flavors

Reading, video, simulation, guided practice, games, visual models, or combinations of several modes.

🧠 💡 🪜
Support Flavors

Visual hints, chunked directions, read-aloud support, modeling, coaching, or reduced scaffolding when appropriate.

🎯 📈 🏆
Mastery Flavors

Immediate feedback, retries, targeted reteaching, progress indicators, challenge levels, and additional practice.

Same standard. Different path.

Students can work toward the same academic goal through different themes, supports, levels of independence, and types of interaction. AI can make that degree of customization much more practical for teachers to build and reuse.

How were these built?

The process begins with instructional design, not code.

STEP 1
Start with the learning

Identify the standard, student need, existing resource, assessment, or instructional challenge.

STEP 2
Design the flavor

Decide how students should experience the learning: context, interactions, supports, feedback, and practice.

STEP 3
Build, test & revise

AI helps build the technology while the teacher tests, adjusts rigor, improves scaffolds, and determines whether the activity supports mastery.
