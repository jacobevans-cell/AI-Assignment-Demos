What happens when we take the curriculum we already teach and rethink how students experience it?

Four working examples of standards-based lessons, practice, scaffolding, and instructional content redesigned with AI.

Built for the way we teach at Explore.

These are not mockups or screenshots. Every example below is a working student activity. GAMIFIED MATH

Decimal Deception

ACTIVITY DESCRIPTION

Students enter a playable space-station adventure built around decimal operations. They choose a character, navigate rooms, avoid moving enemies, restore six Power Crystals by completing math missions, earn points, preserve lives, unlock the portal, and progress into new themed levels.

ARIZONA STANDARD

6.NS.B.3
Fluently add, subtract, multiply, and divide multi-digit decimals using the standard algorithm.

WHAT WAS BUILT
  • Five selectable student characters
  • Six math missions tied to Power Crystals
  • Lives, enemies, scoring, portal progression, and themed levels
  • Math Scanner support and immediate answer feedback
INTENTIONAL DESIGN

The goal was not to make decimal practice easier. It was to make students willing to do more of it. Traditional practice problems became the mechanism for progressing through the game, so the mathematics still controls success.

The worksheet did not disappear. It became the engine of the experience.

INTERACTIVE MULTIMEDIA LESSON

Volleyball Academy

ACTIVITY DESCRIPTION

Students complete eight role-based learning missions covering libero, setter, outside hitter, middle blocker, defensive specialist, bench responsibilities, volleyball basics, and film study. Each mission combines deeper reading, real game situations, multimedia, comprehension checks, and application.

ARIZONA ELA ALIGNMENT

Grades 4–5 RI.1 & RI.7
Use details and evidence from informational text and integrate information presented through text, visual, and multimedia sources.

WHAT WAS BUILT
  • Eight complete role-based instructional missions
  • 5–7 understanding checks within each mission
  • Embedded video, reading sections, multiple choice, and drag-and-drop
  • Saved student progress, IQ scoring, high scores, feedback, and reset tools
INTENTIONAL DESIGN

This began as separate web articles, instructional information, and YouTube videos. Instead of asking students to jump between resources, they were reorganized into one sequenced experience that requires students to read, watch, analyze, answer, sort, and apply information before progressing.

Volleyball is the content. The design model could be used with almost any subject.

GUIDED MATH PRACTICE

Long Division Quest

ACTIVITY DESCRIPTION

Students complete long division directly inside a correctly aligned division structure, one digit and one decision at a time. The program guides them through Divide → Multiply → Subtract → Bring Down while preserving the actual standard algorithm instead of replacing it with multiple choice.

ARIZONA STANDARD

5.NBT.B.6
Find whole-number quotients of whole numbers using strategies based on place value, properties of operations, and the relationship between multiplication and division.

WHAT WAS BUILT
  • Digit-by-digit aligned long-division input
  • Divide, Multiply, Subtract, Bring Down coaching sequence
  • Visual grouping hints showing why a quotient digit works
  • XP, accuracy, streaks, ratings, hint-use tracking, and five-problem missions
INTENTIONAL DESIGN

A worksheet usually tells a student that the final answer is wrong. This system was designed to identify the exact step where the thinking breaks down. Students work in the correct place-value column and receive targeted support without having the problem solved for them.

The scaffold responds to the student's process, not just the final answer.

MULTIMODAL WORD STUDY

Spelling Practice Lab

ACTIVITY DESCRIPTION

One spelling list becomes a complete study-and-practice system. Students can deeply study each word through instructional flash cards, practice the same words through volleyball or soccer gameplay, hear words and sentences aloud, and finish with an independent listen-and-type spelling test.

ARIZONA ELA STANDARDS

4–5.RF.3 & 4–5.L.2
Apply grade-level phonics and word-analysis skills and demonstrate command of conventions, including accurate grade-appropriate spelling.

WHAT WAS BUILT
  • Syllable breakdowns and whole-word pronunciation support
  • Clickable Sound It Out chunks with individual audio
  • Phonograms with multi-letter patterns automatically highlighted
  • “Watch For” spelling hints, examples, visuals, games, and independent testing
INTENTIONAL DESIGN

This was deliberately built beyond simple memorization. The flash cards expose the structure of each word through syllables, sounds, phonograms, patterns, pronunciation, definitions, examples, and targeted reminders. Students then encounter the same words through multiple modes before independent assessment.

See it → hear it → break it apart → study the pattern → practice it → use it → spell it independently.

What could this look like at Explore?

These examples are intentionally different. They show that AI does not require teachers to abandon curriculum or replace instruction. It can be used to rethink the experience surrounding the learning goal.

📄 Worksheet → Experience

Keep the academic problems but surround them with missions, progression, choices, and meaningful feedback.

📚 Resources → Lesson

Turn articles, videos, readings, and teacher-created materials into one organized interactive learning sequence.

🧠 Procedure → Scaffold

Build coaching, models, hints, and immediate feedback directly around the exact step where students need support.

🔤 Practice → Learning System

Take one learning target and create multiple ways to study, hear, analyze, practice, apply, and assess it.

How were these built?

The process begins with instructional design, not code.

STEP 1
Start with the learning

Identify the standard, student need, existing resource, practice problem, lesson, assessment, or instructional challenge.

STEP 2
Design & iterate

Describe the desired student experience. Test each version, identify weaknesses, add scaffolds, change interactions, refine directions, and keep revising.

STEP 3
Teacher remains the designer

AI helps generate and revise the technology, but the teacher determines the learning goal, pedagogy, feedback, difficulty, content, and final classroom experience.
