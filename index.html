What is this?

These are not screenshots or mockups. They are working browser-based activities created with AI and published as simple web pages. The goal is to show how teachers can turn an idea, lesson, review, or practice activity into something students can actually interact with.

GAMIFIED MATH

Decimal Deception

ACTIVITY DESCRIPTION

Students navigate a space-station adventure while solving decimal problems. Correct answers help them complete missions, collect Power Crystals, avoid enemies, and unlock new areas.

ARIZONA MATH STANDARD

6.NS.B.3 — Fluently add, subtract, multiply, and divide multi-digit decimals using the standard algorithm.

INTENTIONAL DESIGN

What if the same practice problems from a worksheet became part of a game? The academic skill remains the focus, but repetitive practice is built into missions, movement, challenges, immediate feedback, and progression.

INTERACTIVE LESSON

Volleyball Academy

ACTIVITY DESCRIPTION

Students work through a series of volleyball missions that combine reading, videos, game situations, multiple-choice comprehension checks, drag-and-drop activities, immediate feedback, scoring, and progress tracking.

LEARNING CONTENT

The lesson was created from existing instructional resources, including web articles, instructional reading, and YouTube videos. Students process the information and apply what they learn to interactive challenges.

INTENTIONAL DESIGN

Instead of simply giving students links to articles and videos, the resources were transformed into one structured interactive assignment. Students read, watch, respond, sort, make decisions, and demonstrate understanding before progressing. Volleyball is simply the example.

How was this made?

The teacher doesn't need to be a programmer. The process starts with describing what students should learn and how the activity should work.

STEP 1
Describe the idea

Give AI the standard, skill, grade level, lesson content, and the type of experience you want students to have.

STEP 2
Build & revise

AI generates the webpage. The teacher tests it and requests changes until the assignment works the way they want.

STEP 3
Publish & share

The finished HTML file is published with GitHub Pages and can be linked or embedded directly into Google Sites.
